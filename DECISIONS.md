# Decisions made without you

One line each, newest at the bottom.

- Kenney packs downloaded fine, so all art is Kenney CC0 plus generated pieces in the same style.
- Mini Characters chosen over Blocky Characters: they share Mini Market's exact colormap and proportions, and ship rigged walk/idle/pick-up/holding clips.
- World unit = one Mini Market tile; other packs are rescaled to it (see docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md) rather than the other way round, because the store is what the camera frames.
- Starter shop is 5x4 tiles plus a 5x2 storeroom: small enough that one shelf, one fridge and a register do not leave a big empty floor.
- Playwright pins `executablePath` to the preinstalled Chromium with SwiftShader so screenshots and perf run headless in CI containers without a GPU.
- Sim tick is 50 ms of game time; the renderer interpolates, so 4x stays smooth without a variable-step sim.
- Fredoka is bundled as a latin-only woff2 (30 KB) plus the full variable TTF in third_party for the record.
- Three.js samples the atlas with `flipY = false` and generated geometry uses glTF's v-down UV convention, so one texture serves both converted models and generated pieces.
- Quantised GLB attributes are expanded to Float32 at load; merging and transforming interleaved or normalised attributes was the source of the "missing neighbourhood" and "exploded people" bugs.
- Character variety is done by shifting colormap strips per triangle at load (outfit permutations, three skin tones), not by extra textures: 13 meshes become 52 looks for free.
- The shop name lives on a free-standing pylon sign by the sidewalk; a fascia sign over the door only shows when the camera looks at the back of the store, because a front fascia hid the whole front row of the floor from the default camera.
- Nature Kit greens are remapped to the town's grass greens at conversion (Kenney's are mint), so trees match roofs and lawns.
- Screenshot runs disable CSS animations: headless SwiftShader renders so slowly that fades were caught half-way.
- Kenney's cash register, standing fridge and end cap have their pivot on the front face; the renderer centres them in their tile with a per-type offset rather than editing the packs.
- The checkout line bends toward the shop's centre and every slot snaps to a walkable cell, so lines never run through a wall.
- Finance lives inside the Stats card and franchises inside Build, so the top bar keeps exactly the seven buttons the brief lists (plus a small Hint button for "what should I do next?").
- Staff work whole days; morale, raises and quitting carry the "imperfect staff" feeling without a shift scheduler.
- Rivals are a daily state machine (open, sale, drift, expand) issued through the same tick rather than a full agent replaying player commands; the balance harness checks they neither crush nor ignore the player.
- Franchises are a lighter daily roll with reports and alerts; their town buildings are stock shop fronts with a name board.
- Guided-player income of about $75/day in week one is the balance anchor: fixtures cost at most five days of that, the shop needs restocking daily, and rivals cost a third of trade at most.
- Shortages drop a line from an order instead of rejecting the order; the first version rejected whole orders and starved the shop.
- `cheat` commands only work in sandbox or under a test harness (`navigator.webdriver`), never in normal play.
- (Rework) The game is played in first person as the manager; the orbit camera survives only for the lobby, the plot picker and screenshots. Pointer lock with a drag-to-look fallback and touch sticks.
- The manager walks in real time whatever the clock speed (the step is divided by the time scale), so 4x speeds the day without making you sprint.
- New shops are a bare concrete room. A setup phase holds the clock until Open shop; deliveries still arrive while you set up (within 20 seconds), and Open shop needs a counter and some stock.
- The storeroom became a delivery pallet inside the back door: you carry every box by hand (E to pick up, E at a shelf to stock). Stockers still carry for you once hired.
- Walls stand outside the floor rectangle (Kenney walls are half a tile thick) and the grid blocks that ring, so fixtures can never clip into a wall.
- The shop got a flat roof and an unlit pale ceiling with light panels; the roof casts no shadow so the sun stands in for the shop lights on the floor. The cinematic camera hides it.
- The floor is one poured slab with saw-cut joints in new neutral greys added to the palette; the Kenney floor tiles read as blue checker.
- The town is built on property lots. Tests assert lots never overlap, every building fits its lot, nothing lands on the shop's plot, and fences stand on lot lines (Kenney fence panels run along x at yaw 0; the old ones were turned 90°).
- Cross streets moved to x = −35 and 39 so all three plots, at their largest lot, fit between them; traffic now uses the same constants (it used to drive down x = 30.5 through gardens).
- The valley is seed-independent (the same mountains, river and fields every game); houses and gardens still vary by seed.
- Traffic appears and leaves at ±92 in the fog. Cars keep a two-unit gap, wait for a gap at junctions, use separate in and out lanes in the driveway, pull into bays nose first and back out. A test drives rush-hour traffic and checks no two cars ever overlap.
- The town is merged in 44-unit chunks with shadow casters apart from flat ground pieces, and the woods are instanced per model and map quarter without shadows: about 200 draw calls and 725k triangles in the widest view.
- The logo was first the in-game manager's head; it is now a shop front (see below).
- The lobby runs a live demo shop at golden hour behind the menu with the camera drifting over Main Street; the menu is styled as shelf-edge labels under a swinging OPEN sign.
- Litter is spills on the floor; dropped baskets lying around read as a bug.
- The balance harness now pays for the fit-out (counter, two shelves, chiller, a first order) and works in person: it scans when someone waits and restocks otherwise, and the delegator hires one person at a time. Starting cash stayed at $800; about $136 is left after the fit-out and a guided player makes about $57 a day in week one.
- People are solid around the player (you bump into shoppers, they step aside) and anyone within 0.3 of the lens is not drawn, so no head ever fills the screen.
- The logo is a shop front: the name on a slate fascia over the yellow shelf bracket, the green-and-white awning, a window with a stocked shelf and a green door with an OPEN sign. Of the options (store front, box, manager head, OPEN sign, shelf) it is the one that says "you run a shop" at a glance and still reads as a shop at 16 px; it repeats the awning the game and the lobby already use. A box reads as shipping, a head as a character game, a sign or a shelf alone as a detail.
- The logo is vector, drawn by `tools/gen-logo.ts` with Fredoka lettering converted to outlines, with a white sticker edge so it reads on the slate top bar and light pages alike. A simpler version (no lettering or small products, heavier outlines) is used at icon sizes. In the lobby the logo is the title, so the separate wordmark went.
- (Second rework) Third person replaces first person: the camera sits 2.5 m behind and above the manager, the crosshair ray passes just over their head, and reach is measured from the manager (1.9 m), not from the lens. Walls between the camera and the manager drop to a ledge only while the manager or the camera is inside the shop; from outside the shop stands whole.
- Shops have no roof or ceiling, so the floor is always readable from the third-person camera.
- The map shrank to a pocket valley: the three shop plots, three rivals, six houses, Main Street and a park, in a level basin about 96 × 41 m ringed by cliffs. Main Street leaves through stone-arched tunnels at x = ±50 ("out to the rest of town"); traffic appears and vanishes inside them, so nothing pops in. Cross streets, Back Lane, farmland and the river valley went.
- The ground is one mesh with shared vertices so lawn colour blends between vertices (mottled, no blotchy triangles); steep faces take one rock colour each so the cliffs keep crisp low-poly facets. Water is vertex-coloured (deep middle, pale edge) with a small shader sparkle.
- Grass tufts, flowers and pebbles are generated geometry, instanced per kind and map half, and sway in a vertex-shader wind; tree leaves sway a little too. Unbuilt plots have gone to wildflowers.
- Animals, birds, butterflies, the fountain spray, the waterfall streaks and the clouds live in `render/Ambient.ts`: render-only, a pure function of time, never in the save. The sky is a gradient dome whose colours follow the lighting.
- The pink-noise ambience bed is gone. Ambience is now short synthesised bird calls by day, crickets at night and the odd duck. Every action has a cue: commands that do not make their own sound get one from a table in `applyCommand`; the runtime adds footsteps, cars whooshing past (panned) and card open/close; every button gets a hover tick and a press pop from one document listener.
- The HUD is icon-first: cash, a day dial and an OPEN/CLOSED sign as pills top left; play/pause/fast as icons top right; the tools as big coloured tiles with a label each on a wooden shelf along the bottom (the brand's shelf), locked tiles showing the day they open. The first day's tips became a checklist ("Today's jobs") with the current job's how-to under it. A Use button bottom right mirrors the crosshair prompt and works on click or tap.
- (Third rework) A follow camera replaces the over-the-shoulder view: a narrow 30° lens looking down from behind and above, 12 units back by default and zooming from 7 (the shelves) to 68 (the whole crossroads). At the default zoom you see the manager, the shelves, the queue and the door; one scroll out adds the lot and the road. The over-the-shoulder camera hid the shop behind the manager and could not show the queue and the rivals together; a tycoon wants to see the whole floor and the corners across the street, and still feel hands-on. Past 30 units the camera stops following and pans, drifting to the middle of the crossroads as it pulls out, so the far limit always frames the town; C comes back.
- Use moved from E to F so Q / E can turn the camera in 45° steps; a right-drag turns it freely and tilts it (35–70°). Old key settings with Use on E move to F. Pointer lock is gone: the mouse is a pointer again (hover shows what a thing is, a click walks there and uses it), and a phone taps to walk or use, drags to turn and pinches to zoom.
- F acts on the nearest usable thing within 1.2 of the manager and in front of them; a click on something further away walks over first. Things you can use win over things you can only look at, so standing between a shelf and the pallet picks whatever you can actually do. A click on a shelf walks to whichever free side of it is nearest, so a shelf turned to face a wall is still stocked from the room.
- The target is outlined on the floor in yellow (white when it only tells you what it is) with the key and the action in a label over it. The crosshair went with first person.
- A docked card or the first day's jobs list pushes the manager to the middle of the world you can still see (measured from the DOM every tenth of a second), and opening Build pulls the camera back until the whole floor fits beside the card, then goes back to your zoom when you close it unless you zoomed in the meantime.
- Building never traps the manager: a fixture is refused on the manager's tile ("You are standing there") and where it would cut them off from the door ("Would box you in").
- The crossroads replaced the valley (the brief): one intersection, the player on one corner, Cheapo, Fresh & Fine and MegaMart on the other three, every rival door, sign and lot in view from mid zoom. The valley spread 725k triangles over scenery nobody visited and hid the competition down the road.
- The backdrop ring the brief asked for (house backs, fences, apartment blocks, trees, mountains) was built and then removed on your instruction: the map is the crossroads and nothing else, on an empty level plain, with both roads running off into the haze so cars drive in out of nowhere and away into nothing. Grass and flowers stay on the verges between the corners only, and the off-map neighbourhoods live on the road signs at the edge of town.
- With no rooftops left, the third billboard stands on tall legs where Elm Street leaves town ("High board on Elm Street", same id and rent). A board on a rival's roof would have floated over an empty lot until that rival opened.
- Performance pass with the new map: rival buildings are plain boxes with window and door panes (their roofs hid the Kenney wall pieces' detail), signs seen from afar use flat lettering without bevels, the verges are one merged mesh, each character is one draw (Kenney's head and body share a skeleton), people and cars sit on instanced blob shadows instead of casting into the shadow map, people out of the camera's view are skipped, and from far off people are instanced stand-ins in their shirt colour. Draw calls went from about 220 to 79–130 and triangles from 700k–990k to 126k–232k.
- Elm Street's three shared bays moved to 3.4 apart (from 2.5) and cars swing in later: a car pulling into the second bay used to swing in beside the car parked in the first and wait there for good. Loading an older save re-seats the bays where they stand now.
- A parked car whose driver no longer exists drives off by itself. At closing time the last customers are sent home, and their parked cars used to stay forever; one of them in a street bay blocked Elm Street and every car behind it (a balance run reached 518 cars).
- The three pickable corners: Main Road corner (north-east: busy, convenience and impulse, $40/day), Elm Street corner (north-west: residential, bargain hunters and loyalists, $30) and Bus stop corner (south-west: commuters and the after-school crowd, impulse, $35). The south-east corner always goes to a rival. South corners are the same shop layout turned half round, so every shop-relative calculation goes through one frame (`toWorld` / `toLocal`).
- The traffic light is a 36-second cycle with an all-walk phase; cars hold at stop lines and walkers wait at the kerb, both reading the same `lightAt(tick)`, so it stays deterministic. A queued car never uses the jam breaker while its light is red.
- Rival customers are visual only: they come from their own `rivalCustomers` random stream and never touch the player's sim. How many there are follows each rival's share of the town's trade (× 0.45, so the crossroads does not drown in people), so a Cheapo sale visibly pulls walkers across the zebra. A driver who finds the player's lot full drives on to the nearest open rival lot ahead, a free Elm Street bay, or out of town.
- MegaMart's extra branches and the player's franchises open in four off-map neighbourhoods (Hillside, East End, Elm Heights, Riverside), shown on the road signs at the edge of town; flyers go out to the same neighbourhoods. Old franchise plots map to off-map sites and old plots to corners (save v9).
- Anyone who leaves the world is taken out of every till's line straight away. Closing time used to remove the last customers while their ids stayed in the queue, so the next morning the line waited on someone who no longer existed and nobody was served all day (a balance run went from 75 served to 0).
- Toy-town art direction: warm and saturated, never grey. The shop floor is a cream checker lino, the walls inside are banded in the shop's colour (skirting, a dark lower band, a cream rail, a pale upper band), fixtures are painted wood, cream and pale blue instead of Kenney's greys, and the light is warmer in the morning and evening. Colours are repainted per colormap strip at load (`paintStrips`), so no new textures.
- Six player colour schemes (Fresh green, Berry, Tangerine, Plum, Teal, Cocoa), picked beside the shop's name. None is near a rival's colour (Cheapo yellow and red, Fresh & Fine deep green and cream, MegaMart blue and white): a test keeps every player primary at least 60 RGB steps from every rival's, so bags always tell stores apart. Old saves become Fresh green (save v10).
- Shopping bags carry the store's colour: one to three by how much was bought, held by everyone walking or driving away from a till, Cheapo's and the others' shoppers included. Drivers stop at the boot for a moment to load them. Far-off people (the instanced stand-ins) keep a coloured block for their bags so the overview still shows who is winning.
- Rival shoppers you can see went from 0.45 to 0.7 of each rival's trade, so a rival's bags are as easy to spot as ours. They are still visual only and never touch the player's sim.
- Reaction bubbles (too pricey, out of stock, long queue, great deal, love this place) are sim events in a small ring buffer (`state.reactions`, 16 long), drawn as at most four sprites at once. One person cannot react twice in six seconds, and the good ones (deal, love) at most one every five seconds town-wide, so bubbles mean something.
- Props are placed by a pure function (`cornerProps`): each one takes the free lawn spot nearest its anchor, never on a path, the paving, the building or a bay (a test walks every corner and shop size). Bigger shops get more of them; opening days get bunting, rival sales get sale posters.
- Bloom only catches things meant to glow: lit windows, signs and lamps are given colours brighter than 1, above the bloom threshold, and nothing lit by the sun gets there. A pass before the bloom zeroes any NaN pixel, because one bad pixel smeared by the blur blanked whole frames. Tilt-shift fades in only past 34 units of zoom. Both are off on Low.
- Buying a closed rival's corner makes it one of your branches, not a second simulated floor. The building goes up in your colours and under your name, its shoppers walk in and out carrying your bags, and it reports every night like the branches out of town (a manager runs it by your rules, sharing your cash and reputation). A full second floor would mean a second pathfinding grid, staff split between shops, stock routed between two pallets and a camera walking between them: twice the sim's busiest code for the last goal on the ladder.
- The goal ladder follows the brief's first pass, tuned with the balance harness. Goal 4 asks for a $120 night (not $150): with a cashier and a stocker on the payroll a well-run 7×7 shop nets $100–200, and $150 left some seeds waiting a week for a lucky Saturday. Goal 5 asks for a third of the crossroads' shoppers over the last week (not 40% for seven days running): with three rivals a third makes you the busiest store, a strong 7×7 shop with the doubled lot holds 33–45%, and a daily 40% bar was decided by rival sales and Saturdays rather than by how well the shop ran. The quiet first days (fewer than two rivals trading) never count toward the week.
- Rivals' shoppers in the night card's shares and in their books come from one model on the player's scale: the town pool by customer type, the corner's traffic and mix, the same softmax share, awareness and reputation the way the player's own count, and the arrivals-per-expected factor (1.46) the player's spawner runs at. The player's side is the real count: everyone who came, less drivers turned away from a full lot.
- Rival books are small numbers on purpose: a basket of $3.60–5.40 is what a shopper spends per visit at the crossroads (the player's own takings run $2–4 per arrival), and fixed costs grow with the town, so a rival's profit tracks its share rather than the calendar. Cheapo closes in weeks 3–4 against a strong player, Fresh & Fine late in week 6 on one seed in six, MegaMart never (its branches pay $40 a day each toward the crossroads store).
- Out-selling Cheapo compares today's takings with Cheapo's modelled sales; a closed Cheapo counts as beaten.
- Rival moves and the day's events are rolled at close (into `state.tomorrow`) and applied when the day opens, from the same random streams in the same order, so no seeded test expectation moved. A save loaded at night without a plan rolls one at opening.
- Branches out of town are now a reward (the Market hall goal). Old saves that already have branches keep them and keep the unlock.
- The night card's costs are goods, rent and power, staff, and loans and shoplifting: the lines the day's profit is made of. Ads, billboards and card fees still come straight out of cash and do not count against the day's profit (as before).
- The optional side goal was left out: one goal at a time keeps the chip short and the ladder readable.
- A challenger that takes over an empty corner wears sky blue (#29B6D8), kept 60+ RGB steps from every player scheme like the three rivals' colours.
- Traffic gets a last-resort jam breaker: a car backing out goes after 30 seconds of waiting, and any car held up for 40 seconds (never by a red light) creeps through. Forty-day balance runs gridlocked MegaMart's double lot on busy days: cars backing out waited for the aisle, and the cars in the aisle waited for them.
- The balance harness's guided player now plays the way the goals and tips teach over forty days: it reorders when the pallet runs low (the truck is there in a minute and a half), orders new lines for empty shelves, moves up to the 7×7 shop when it can keep $200 for stock, doubles the lot once drivers are turned away, then hires a stocker. Its week-one profit went from ≈ $63 to ≈ $94 a day, past the brief's ≈ $75. The other strategies play twelve days (no rival may close before day 12), with going bust judged at day eight as before. Games run in parallel processes (about four minutes on four cores).
